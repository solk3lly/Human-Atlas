# Human Atlas anatomical accuracy review

Reviewed at the initial pre-remediation baseline on 25 November 2026.

## Verdict

**The atlas is approximately life-sized, and several major structures have plausible adult dimensions. Its geometry, coverage, and labels are too uneven to support a claim of anatomical accuracy across the whole model.** The shipped skeleton spans about 169.6 cm from its lowest to highest vertex. Both femurs span about 45.6 cm, the kidneys about 10.6–10.8 cm, and the trachea about 10.8 cm. The ureters have plausible lengths but exaggerated upper diameters, the cardiac model omits major teaching structures, and entire sets of geometry are missing or duplicated.

I inspected every shipped mesh for packing errors and exact duplicates, measured selected structures and anatomical relationships, reviewed the viewer and generation code, and compared measurements with primary anatomy studies and seven Visible Human male reference assets. Absolute measurements assume one atlas unit is one metre. The project does not record a physical unit, a source OBJ version and checksum, or a per-structure provenance table. The evidence supports the metre interpretation, but the precise size and shape of the original donor cannot be reconstructed from this checkout. ## Size and shape measurements

An end-to-end span is the largest distance between two vertices. Principal-axis measurements are projected extents along the mesh's dominant axes and are sensitive to mesh sampling. Tube lengths are sums of distances between successive cross-section centres, computed where indexed topology gave a consistent sequence of complete 12-vertex rings. Volume is reported for closed meshes with consistently oriented edges. Measurements are rounded to reflect the available evidence.

| Structure | Measured atlas geometry | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Skeleton and long bones | Skeleton height 169.6 cm. Femurs 456 mm each, tibiae 358 mm each, humeri 320 mm each, measured end to end. | Adult-scale. One adult bone series reported means of 463 mm for the femur and 370 mm for the tibia, using its own landmarks. The underlying TARO height published by NICT is 172.8 cm. Skin, posture, and export history prevent treating the difference as scale error. [Bone series](https://boneandjoint.org.uk/article/10.1302/0301-620X.79B6.0791019/pdf) · [NICT TARO description](https://www2.nict.go.jp/emc/bio/en-data-offer/information-on-the-provision.html) |
| Kidneys | Left 107.8 mm and right 106.0 mm end to end. The right kidney's uppermost vertex is 19 mm lower. Closed-shell volumes are about 104 and 107 mL. | Gross length and laterality are plausible. A 1,040-adult CT series measured left and right means of 111.3 and 108.5 mm pole to pole. The Visible Human male kidney capsules have projected long-axis spans of 123.9 and 125.4 mm, with transverse principal spans of 75.8 × 49.2 and 62.2 × 45.0 mm. The atlas kidney spans are 107.7 × 56.5 × 36.1 and 105.8 × 62.6 × 33.1 mm. The differences show why plausible length alone leaves the contour and thickness unverified. - **Packing and mapping.** All 15 available bundles decode, every mesh's indices are in range, and every mesh is referenced by exactly one sidebar item within its layer. Some sidebar items intentionally group both sides or split chunks. The largest object chunk has 50,530 vertices. I counted 115 zero-area triangles across 11 meshes and 3,471 repeated geometric triangles within meshes. Most repeats are in the ascending pharyngeal arteries. The separately counted 273 exact copies are whole meshes within a layer. The 16-bit quantization step is 0.0025–0.0259 mm across shipped layers under the metre interpretation, so rounding is too small to explain the major size discrepancies. [Packed data](data/) · [Decoder](index.html#L660)
- **Surface fidelity.** After merging identically quantized coordinates for inspection, both simplified femurs, the trachea, pleura, and all numbered liver segments have boundary edges, edges shared by more than two faces, or both. Open edges can be intentional for a membrane, vessel, cut surface, or chamber opening. These counts establish which surfaces should not be treated as closed solids and cannot identify which defects predate export. The right femur alone has 17 boundary edges and 35 edges used by more than two faces despite its plausible length. [Skeleton simplifier](tools/dec.py#L3) · [Shared simplifier](tools/dec2.py#L3)
- **Readme and model coverage.** The Nerves layer contains 120 sidebar items, but only two have description cards. The Muscles layer has cards for six of 211 items. The remaining entries get the viewer's placeholder. The nerve assets include spinal white matter and anterior and posterior horns, but the layer has no brain or cerebellum and no separate whole spinal cord entry. The endocrine hypothalamus is a separate model. There is no female variant in this checkout. [Content and mapping](index.html#L639) · [Description fallback](index.html#L716) · [Nerve generation](tools/gen2.py#L67) · [Disabled menu option](index.html#L609)
- **Regeneration risk.** The selection scripts depend on a particular object order, and a fresh export can reorder objects. The urinary and heart scripts deduplicate with array shape and a rounded sum of coordinates, without comparing faces or full coordinates, so the key can merge anatomically different same-sized objects. Current bundle inspection cannot establish whether this has already happened. Use a pinned source and compare position and face data when removing duplicates. [Urinary generator](tools/gen6.py#L11) · [Heart generator](tools/gen8.py#L62) · [Source availability](README.en.md#adding-a-new-system-or-structure)

## Reference models and validation

BodyParts3D used a whole-body MRI dataset with 2 mm intervals as a starting point. Its authors document that medical illustrators filled in missing details and refined blurred boundaries using anatomical references. Z-Anatomy is a derivative atlas, and this repository performs another round of selection, simplification, and packing. A fine encoding grid preserves detail in the supplied surface but cannot recover details that the source scan or the artists never captured. The exact source export and per-object history are especially important when deciding whether to repair a mesh locally or replace it. [BodyParts3D paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkn613) · [Z-Anatomy source](https://github.com/Z-Anatomy/Models-of-human-anatomy)

| Source | Best use in this atlas | What to account for |
|---|---|---|
| HuBMAP Human Reference Atlas | Expert-reviewed male and female organ references with explicit structures, anatomical IDs, and an approval workflow. Good first comparison for the heart, airway, kidneys, and liver segments. | Reference organ surfaces can represent different anatomical boundaries, such as capsule versus cortex, and may be edited or assembled. Verify the object version, node semantics, pose, and transforms before overlaying it. [HRA paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10079270/) · [Approval SOP](https://zenodo.org/records/10358858) |
| NLM Visible Human Project | Underlying cross-sectional images for checking structure boundaries, relative organ positions, and both sexes. | Two donors. Cadaver geometry and slice acquisition conditions affect comparisons with in vivo imaging. [NLM dataset](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/research/visible/index.html) |
| TotalSegmentator annotated CT data | Multi-subject comparisons for major bones, organs, muscles, and selected vessels. Useful for estimating population variation and identifying size outliers. | The clinical dataset includes pathologies and different scan protocols. Select appropriate cases and verify reference labels and contours at the level needed for the atlas. [Primary study and dataset links](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.230024) |
| ESRF Human Organ Atlas | Donor-specific, high-resolution internal organ detail for local reference checks, such as the kidney or heart. | Organ samples and preparation conditions matter. Use the donor and scan metadata when comparing shapes, and use a whole-body reference for placement. [Primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12978218/) · [Source datasets](https://human-organ-atlas.esrf.fr/) |

1. Record source revision and object ID, units, coordinate convention, transform, specimen and pose where known, mesh derivation, and anatomical ID for each model. Preserve physical spacing and orientation from the source images. A metric ruler or inspector should use that recorded calibration. [DICOM Image Plane Module](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_c.7.6.2.html) · [glTF coordinate and unit specification](https://registry.khronos.org/glTF/specs/2.0/glTF-2.0.html#coordinate-system-and-units) · [FIPAT anatomical terminology](https://ifaa.net/committees/anatomical-terminology-fipat/)
2. Restore or accurately describe coverage before revising shapes. Prioritize the lymphatic load failure, the superior vena cava and missing valve structures, the bilateral airway cartilages, and the GI endpoints. Correct vessel regions and side tags using one mapping that drives both the sidebar and the viewer.
3. Compare each replacement in a shared anatomical coordinate frame. Use homologous landmarks and distances in millimetres for bones and organ placement. For ducts and vessels use branch identity, centreline connectivity, and diameters at named points. For solid organs use volumes and point-to-surface errors after registration. Compare the original and exported meshes with the same metrics, and choose tolerances from reference resolution, inter-reader agreement, and the intended teaching use. [Metrics Reloaded validation guidance](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-02151-z)
4. Have an anatomist review representative views, connections, laterality, and description cards. Use the Visible Human and CT comparisons to document where the model represents one specimen, a simplified teaching form, or a known anatomical variant. Retain a release-level list of omissions.

## Verification and manual review

I decoded the base64 buffers as little-endian `uint16`, checked offsets and indices, compared triangle coordinates within each layer, and calculated the measurements directly from decoded vertices. Reference measurements use the HuBMAP `ccf-releases/v1.1` Visible Human male GLBs and apply the full node transforms. The measurements are independent comparisons across models. No same-specimen CT/MRI or original Z-Anatomy OBJ was available for registered surface error analysis. I did not certify every attachment, branch, surface, or clinical description. [Reference GLB files](https://github.com/hubmapconsortium/ccf-releases/tree/main/v1.1/models)

The only repository addition is this report. CodeScene and Codacy are inapplicable to this review because there are no code changes. The checks above inspected data and viewer math. No browser session or UI test ran for this review.

For manual review in the app, open **Sistem Limfatik** and check whether a deployment actually serves the missing asset. Open **Sistem Pernapasan**, turn off **Konteks**, and inspect each of the four paired cartilage structures on both sides. In **Toraks**, select a left lung artery and compare its side tag with **Kanan** and **Kiri**. Open **Rangka → Tampilkan semua** and then isolate the incudes on a portrait canvas. A corrected viewer should keep the skull and both selected bones in frame, and should respond consistently when a sidebar item is hidden by the current side filter.
